  1. A one-line input, not a 4-line textarea. A link is one line (m05 §2); it grows into a textarea only when what's pasted has a space or new line (the M5 rule, kept).
  2. Paste button: one tap instead of tap–long-press–Paste (C17, readText() on tap only). Pasting a link pinches it at once: Paste is the confirmation. Saves two of the six steps (plan §2 N1).
  3. Screenshots are the fallback, so a quiet link, not a second big button (today "Or add screenshots" competes with Pinch it).
  4. The queue lives with capture, under a count: items left, no fake percentage (C6). The library only shows a one-line "ready" link.
  5. A ready draft is the strongest row: its picture, the title the model found, the whole row a link ("Check it ›"), so Pinch it stays the one filled button (ui-craft: one primary per view) (today a dashed box with a raw URL, m05 §4).
  6. The picture appears as soon as the worker has it (it's saved with the fetching step, design §5), so the row is recognisable within seconds, before the draft exists. Stage words, not a spinner (A14, A16: "stuck loading").
  7. A failure names what wasn't found and offers the next path: "no recipe" (no retry helps, D23) → Paste its text; a fetch that failed three times → Retry (desktop shows both) (C7 Expensify; A11 "recipe in comments").
  8. Confirmation without a modal: a toast with Undo, 5 s (C5 Gmail), the one way to take it back; kept although the row appears too, because the user named "no confirmation" as a pain. The field empties and keeps focus for the next link.
  9. Desktop: capture left, queue right, both visible at once: the user pastes several links from a laptop session and watches them land.
  10. ⌘V anywhere on the page pinches (desktop only; a paste event on the document, ignored while typing in another field).
